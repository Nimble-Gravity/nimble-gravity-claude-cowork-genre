# Voice profile

## How I want drafts to sound
- **Bottom line first.** Lead with what the model does and the headline risks, then the detail.
- **Formality:** clear and practitioner-grade — structured docs for the platform team, plainer for a stand-up note.
- **Sentence feel:** short and declarative. Say what you mean; cut filler.
- **Tone:** measured and precise. State what works and what's broken evenly; never dramatic.
- **Specific over general:** name the line, the cell, the function — not "the code has issues."

## Jargon and word choice
- **Use** precise engineering terms: input, transformation, output, hardcoded, side effect, edge case, business rule.
- **Avoid** hype or vague judgment ("messy," "terrible," "great") unless backed by a specific finding, and marketing language of any kind.

## Format defaults
- A structured document with clear headers — purpose → components → inputs → logic walk-through → defects & risks → open questions — not long prose.
- One page for a stand-up summary; as long as needed for a spec, but never padded.

## A good sample in my voice
"This workbook prices property treaty layers from a pasted loss run. Three risks before we port it:
the row count on LossRun!B1 is maintained by hand, the 5% trend constant hasn't been revisited since
2019, and the rate-on-line cell reads B4 where the limit actually lives in B3. Full walk-through and
open questions below — nothing here is guessed."
